Flow first look: where delivered work flows

What this page shows

This repository automates how my machines are built (Ansible). I build it together with AI coding agents, and I also use it as a lab for learning how to work with agents. This page looks back at the work that was delivered each month and asks where it went:

  • Machines: work on the machine automation itself (roles, playbooks, CI, scripts).
  • Lab: work on how the agents work: their rules, skills and settings (for example .claude/, AGENTS.md).
  • Rework: fixes and reverts of earlier delivered work. This is failure demand: work that exists only because earlier work was not right.
  • Automated: dependency updates opened by Dependabot. Discarded: pull requests closed without merging.

"Delivered" means a pull request merged into main, or a commit made directly on main. Every item counts once, whatever its size. A change is sorted into machines or lab by the majority of the files it touches; a title starting with "fix", "bug" or "revert" makes it rework. Data covers 2026-04-01 to 2026-11-20 (git snapshot 0455604). Earlier months are left out, because before April most work went straight to main and the counts are not comparable.

The question behind it: my own attention is probably the bottleneck of this way of working, and rework probably eats a large part of it. This first look tests the cheap part of that idea with data that already exists.

How to read

  • Signals come only from the D8 p-chart verdicts below; otherwise the answer is "no signal". Never read trends by eye.
  • 2026-11 is partial (snapshot 2026-11-20) and is left out of the trend verdicts.
  • A count is not effort: large and small items count the same.
  • chore is excluded only for direct commits; the 4 chore PRs are counted.
  • Rework counts are candidates: fixes also repair external breakage.
  • Discarded counts closed, unmerged PRs only: a lower bound, counted, not judged.

Is anything changing? Trend verdicts

Monthly shares jump around by chance, especially in months with few items. To avoid reading a trend into noise, each share is judged by a fixed rule chosen before looking at the data: a p-chart (Wheeler). It computes the average share over all readable months (p̄) and, for each month, a band of normal variation that is wider when the month has fewer items. A real change shows as a month outside its band, or as 6 rises or 6 falls in a row. Anything else is "no signal": the months differ, but not more than chance would explain. Only full months with at least 5 items are read (2026-04 to 2026-10); the 6-in-a-row rule needs at least 7 such months.

  • Rework share: Is rework (failure demand) growing or shrinking? The share counts rework items among all delivered non-automated items. Average p̄ = 17.5% (22 of 126). Verdict: no signal: every readable month stays inside its band; the 6-in-a-row rule could not fire with only 5 readable months. Months too small to read: 2026-07, 2026-08.
  • My share: Am I opening or committing less of the work myself? The share counts items I opened or committed among all delivered non-automated items. Average p̄ = 27.8% (35 of 126). Verdict: no signal: every readable month stays inside its band; the 6-in-a-row rule could not fire with only 5 readable months. Months too small to read: 2026-07, 2026-08.
  • Agent rework share: Does handing work to agents come back as rework? (the balancing check for my share) The share counts rework items among the items agents opened or committed. Average p̄ = 18.7% (17 of 91). Verdict: no signal: every readable month stays inside its band; the 6-in-a-row rule could not fire with only 5 readable months. Months too small to read: 2026-07, 2026-08.
